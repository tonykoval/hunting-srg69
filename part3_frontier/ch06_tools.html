Chapter 6 · Part III Tools & Reproducibility

Every closure in this book is a computation, and every computation is public. Here is the engine and the exact commands that produced each result — and the ones that would push the two open regions to a verdict.

6.1   The engine

All of it lives in orbit-gen — an orbit-matrix enumerator and lifter, a SAT-modulo-symmetries solver, a partial-difference-set search, and a Gram-PSD / Euclidean filter. The prescribed-automorphism sweep runs through the search binary.

6.2   The odd-prime sweep (proven empty)

# No order-3 automorphism — all fixed-point counts f, orbit counts o=16..23
for f in 0 3 6 9 12 15 18 21; do
  search 69 20 7 5 3 --f $f --timeout 86400        # each → 0 matrices
done

# No order-5 automorphism
search 69 20 7 5 5 --f 9 --timeout 3600            # 0 matrices
search 69 20 7 5 5 --f 4 --timeout 3600            # 0 matrices

6.3   The order-23 SAT

# No order-23 automorphism — 69 = 3·23, prescribed Z23, decided directly
search 69 20 7 5 23 --semiregular --emit-cnf | kissat     # UNSAT in ~23 s

6.4   The Cayley / PDS closure

# Only Z69 exists at order 69; no (69,20,7,5) partial difference set
pds_anneal 69 20 7 5 --group Z69 --restarts 500000        # best viol 48, none
pds_cnf    69 20 7 5 --group Z69 | kissat                 # cyclic closure UNSAT (9 s)

6.5   The open $p=2$ sweep (distributed)

# Involutions: f odd, o=(69-f)/2 = 25..34; millions–billions of cubes
search 69 20 7 5 2 --f 19 --timeout 604800               # 4.6 M cubes — one box
# Split a case across machines (any non-empty result = a liftable skeleton):
CUBE_RANGE=0-500000   search 69 20 7 5 2 --f 19 &        # node A
CUBE_RANGE=500000-1e6 search 69 20 7 5 2 --f 19 &        # node B  ...
bash scripts/distribute_srg.sh srg69 2 19 pc-a:8 pc-b:8   # per-machine commands

6.6   The rigid Gram search

# Sound rank-23 PSD pruning on the 5-eigenspace embedding (Chapter 4)
srg69-extension --vseq --gram --rank 23 --timeout 3600
#   sound: never rejects a real graph; per-candidate tree > 4.3 M nodes — cluster-scale.

6.7   The standing invitation

Push a region, settle the question

Finish the involution sweep ($\S$6.5) with zero matrices, or exhaust the rigid Gram search ($\S$6.6), and $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ is refuted — the smallest open existence question closed. A single lifted matrix or completed graph proves it exists — the first ever on these parameters. The recipe is here; the cores are the only thing missing.
