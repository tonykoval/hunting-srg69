Chapter 5 · Part III Findings & the Decision

The hunt has not settled existence — but it has carved the possibility space down to two well-defined regions and a clean decision procedure. Here is what is proven, what remains, and exactly what either ending requires.

5.1   What is proven

Each of these is a verifiable, exhaustive result — every search ran to a real EXHAUSTED / UNSAT certificate, not a timeout:

ResultHow
No automorphism of order $3$orbit matrices, all $f$ ($o=16$–$23$): $0$ matrices
No automorphism of order $5$orbit matrices, $f=4,9$: $0$ matrices
No automorphism of order $\ge 7$orbit matrices: $0$ matrices
No automorphism of order $23$prescribed-$\mathbb{Z}_{23}$ SAT: UNSAT ($23$ s)
No Cayley graphonly $\mathbb{Z}_{69}$; no PDS (SA + SAT closure)
Attribution correction (2026-07-11) — most odd-order exclusions duplicate the literature

A publication check (run for the sibling Conway-99 write-up) showed that Behbahani & Lam, Discrete Math. 311 (2011) already proved "only prime orders $2$ and $3$ possible" for $(69,20,7,5)$ — their published runs cover $p = 5,7,11,13,17,19$ and $23$ (the $p=23$ case via $2$ orbit matrices that fail expansion). The table above therefore records independent verifications by different methods (their code was never published), not first proofs — with one genuine exception: our order-$3$ exclusion covers all admissible fixed-point counts, strengthening the picture at $p=3$. The standing theorem below survives unchanged, but its odd-order pillar cites Behbahani–Lam first and this project's re-derivations second. The genuinely new territory at these parameters remains exactly the $p=2$ block and the rigid core.

Standing theorem

Any $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$, if it exists, has no automorphism of odd order and is not a Cayley graph. Its automorphism group is a $2$-group — and once the $p=2$ sweep closes, it would have to be trivial.

5.2   What remains — two regions

The unresolved space is exactly two pieces, both cluster-scale:

  • Involution-symmetric graphs ($p=2$). The order-$2$ orbit-matrix sweep: $f\in\{1,\dots,19\}$ odd, $o=25$–$34$, hundreds of millions of cubes in the $f=9$–$19$ band and billions overall, with no fixed-block dedup. Embarrassingly parallel via CUBE_RANGE splitting.
  • Rigid graphs. The full-matrix search with the sound Gram-PSD / Euclidean rank-$23$ filter — per-candidate trees beyond $4.3$ M nodes, the complete triangle-neighbourhood enumeration larger still.

5.3   The decision procedure

Two endings

Existence. Any $p=2$ orbit matrix that lifts, or any adjacency matrix the Gram search completes, is a genuine $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ — the first graph on these parameters, resolving the smallest open existence question in Brouwer's table.
Non-existence. The $p=2$ sweep returns $0$ matrices and the rigid enumeration exhausts with no graph — then $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ does not exist, a refutation in the line of Shpectorov–Zhao's $(85,14,3,2)$.

Either way the parameters are settled. A timeout settles nothing — both regions need real exhaustion certificates.

5.4   The distributed plan

Both remaining regions are compute, not insight. The plan:

  1. Distribute the $p=2$ sweep. Split each $(f,o)$ case's cube range across machines (CUBE_RANGE); any non-empty result is a liftable skeleton. Clearing all $f$ proves "no nontrivial automorphism".
  2. Distribute the rigid Gram search. Hand the triangle-neighbourhood candidates to cluster nodes at hour-plus per-candidate budgets; the PSD filter prunes each soundly.
  3. Watch for a witness. A single lift or completion ends the hunt immediately and positively.

5.5   New levers from the SRG(37) campaign  (2026-07)

In July 2026 the sibling hunt for a $6803$rd $\mathrm{SRG}(37,18,8,9)$ turned its "cluster-scale" residual into a single-desktop campaign. Both unresolved regions here are the same shape of workload, and three levers transfer directly (untested on $69$ — that is the point of listing them):

  • The full static lex chain. On the two-graph(38) SMS search, the canonicity propagator turned out to cost $0.4\%$ of wall — the wall was raw CDCL depth. Encoding the row-lex ordering over all consecutive row pairs as static, unit-propagatable clauses (not a truncated chain, not propagator-only) gave a measured $50$–$70\times$ on hard cubes, with the enumerated model set verified bit-identical (implied-clause soundness). Any row-lex symmetry-broken encoding of the $p=2$ sweep or the rigid Gram search should try this transfer before buying more cores.
  • Recursive adaptive cube-and-conquer. Queue of $(\text{cube},\text{size})$ nodes, $600$-second budget each, timeout ⇒ push $16$ finer children. Measured on SRG(37): $15/16$ children of a hard cube exhaust in ${\sim}13$ s, so the hard core isolates itself $16\times$ tighter per level unattended. The billions-of-cubes $f=9$–$19$ band is exactly the workload this replaces — the CUBE_RANGE splitting stays, but no cube is ever a $24$-hour monolith again. Measured limit (2026-07-13): on SRG(37)'s own terminal residue the shed rate collapses to ${\sim}11\%$ and splitting grows the hard set — expect the recursion to grind the bulk and then hit a wall class that needs a different idea, not more depth.
  • Deep-pinned GPU population search. A defect-minimising SLS ($56{,}000$ candidates/s at $n=38$ on a desktop GPU) hunting inside the surviving cubes of the exact search rather than from random starts. For these parameters the fitness is $\lVert A^2 - 2A - 5J - 15I\rVert^2$ (the defect against $A^2 = 15I + 2A + 5J$), with the orbit-matrix or cube-fixed entries pinned. Find-only — a free parallel shot at the witness while the CPUs chase certificates.

And one derivable analogue: the feasible-characteristic-polynomial endgame tables (Greaves–Syatriadi LP enumeration, used on the conference-matrix side). With integer spectrum $\{20,\,5^{23},\,(-3)^{45}\}$, Cauchy interlacing pins growing powers of $(x-5)$ and $(x+3)$ in every large principal submatrix, leaving a small integer cofactor to enumerate — a sound deep-node filter for whichever enumeration reaches the rigid core.

$\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ is the smallest door in the SRG tables still closed. The key is no longer a new idea — it is enough cores pointed at two precisely-bounded enumerations, with a sound filter on each. This book is the map of which doors are already nailed shut and which one is left to push.
