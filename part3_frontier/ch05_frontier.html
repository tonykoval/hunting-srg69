Chapter 5 · Part III Findings & the Decision

The hunt has not settled existence — but it has carved the possibility space down to two well-defined regions and a clean decision procedure. Here is what is proven, what remains, and exactly what either ending requires.

5.1   What is proven

Each of these is a verifiable, exhaustive result — every search ran to a real EXHAUSTED / UNSAT certificate, not a timeout:

ResultHow
No automorphism of order $3$orbit matrices, all $f$ ($o=16$–$23$): $0$ matrices
No automorphism of order $5$orbit matrices, $f=4,9$: $0$ matrices
No automorphism of order $\ge 7$orbit matrices: $0$ matrices
No automorphism of order $23$prescribed-$\mathbb{Z}_{23}$ SAT: UNSAT ($23$ s)
No Cayley graphonly $\mathbb{Z}_{69}$; no PDS (SA + SAT closure)
Standing theorem

Any $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$, if it exists, has no automorphism of odd order and is not a Cayley graph. Its automorphism group is a $2$-group — and once the $p=2$ sweep closes, it would have to be trivial.

5.2   What remains — two regions

The unresolved space is exactly two pieces, both cluster-scale:

  • Involution-symmetric graphs ($p=2$). The order-$2$ orbit-matrix sweep: $f\in\{1,\dots,19\}$ odd, $o=25$–$34$, hundreds of millions of cubes in the $f=9$–$19$ band and billions overall, with no fixed-block dedup. Embarrassingly parallel via CUBE_RANGE splitting.
  • Rigid graphs. The full-matrix search with the sound Gram-PSD / Euclidean rank-$23$ filter — per-candidate trees beyond $4.3$ M nodes, the complete triangle-neighbourhood enumeration larger still.

5.3   The decision procedure

Two endings

Existence. Any $p=2$ orbit matrix that lifts, or any adjacency matrix the Gram search completes, is a genuine $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ — the first graph on these parameters, resolving the smallest open existence question in Brouwer's table.
Non-existence. The $p=2$ sweep returns $0$ matrices and the rigid enumeration exhausts with no graph — then $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ does not exist, a refutation in the line of Shpectorov–Zhao's $(85,14,3,2)$.

Either way the parameters are settled. A timeout settles nothing — both regions need real exhaustion certificates.

5.4   The distributed plan

Both remaining regions are compute, not insight. The plan:

  1. Distribute the $p=2$ sweep. Split each $(f,o)$ case's cube range across machines (CUBE_RANGE); any non-empty result is a liftable skeleton. Clearing all $f$ proves "no nontrivial automorphism".
  2. Distribute the rigid Gram search. Hand the triangle-neighbourhood candidates to cluster nodes at hour-plus per-candidate budgets; the PSD filter prunes each soundly.
  3. Watch for a witness. A single lift or completion ends the hunt immediately and positively.

$\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ is the smallest door in the SRG tables still closed. The key is no longer a new idea — it is enough cores pointed at two precisely-bounded enumerations, with a sound filter on each. This book is the map of which doors are already nailed shut and which one is left to push.
