Chapter 3 · Part II The Automorphism Sweep

Historical: superseded by the nonexistence theorem, Ch. 7

This chapter was written while the existence of $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ was open, and is kept as the record of the hunt. On 2026-09-26 the question was settled: no such graph exists (Chapter 7; computer-assisted proof, write-up under review). The order-$3$ re-derivation and the $p=2$ sweep described here as running were never finished. The theorem makes them unnecessary: a graph that does not exist has no automorphisms.

Run the orbit-matrix engine of Chapter 2 at every prime order $p$ and every admissible fixed-point count $f$. The result so far is a wall of zeros — and one sharp theorem at order $23$. What remains is a single prime, $p=2$, and it is enormous.

3.1   Odd primes: a wall of zeros

The fixed-point bound ($\mathrm{fix}\le 32$) and $f\equiv 69 \pmod p$ leave finitely many $(p,f)$. Every odd-prime case has been enumerated to exhaustion, and every one returns zero orbit matrices. Filter the runs below:

Theorem (no automorphism of order $5$ or any prime $\ge 7$)

No $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ admits an automorphism of order $5$ or of any prime order $\ge 7$. Both $f$ for $p=5$ and every admissible $(p,f)$ at the larger primes yield $0$ orbit matrices under complete enumeration. $\square$

Correction (2026-08-21): the $p=3$ row is not a theorem yet

An earlier version of this section stated the order-$3$ exclusion as proven. That claim came from the pre-audit enumeration engine, whose diagonal handling was later shown to silently drop matrices at odd primes — precisely the class of "zero" this project no longer accepts. The order-$3$ case is the live campaign of this book: the fleet is re-deriving it root by root with the audited engine, and the question stays open until every lane carries a completeness certificate (or a hit). Independent context: Behbahani's 2009 thesis (Thm 4.2) pins the full prime frontier at $\{2,3\}$ — excluding $5$ and everything larger, but explicitly retaining $3$. Our wall of zeros at $5$ and $\ge 7$ was re-verified against those tables this week ($p=17,19$ re-run independently in seconds at the prototype layer). What remains open is exactly $p \in \{2, 3\}$: the two chapters that follow.

3.2   The order-23 case, settled directly

Order $23$ is special: $69 = 3\cdot 23$, so an order-$23$ automorphism acts with $f\equiv 69\equiv 0 \pmod{23}$ fixed points — either $0$ or $23$. The structure is rigid enough to attack as a direct SAT instance rather than a full orbit-matrix sweep, and it falls almost instantly.

Theorem (no order-23 automorphism)

No $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ has an automorphism of order $23$. The prescribed-$\mathbb{Z}_{23}$ existence formula is UNSAT, decided by kissat in $23$ seconds. $\square$

3.3   The remaining prime: $p=2$

Only involutions are left. An order-$2$ automorphism fixes an odd number of points, $f\in\{1,3,\dots,19\}$, with $o=\tfrac{69-f}{2}$ orbits — so $o$ ranges over $25$–$34$, far larger than the odd-prime cases, and the cube counts explode:

The $f=9$–$19$ band alone exceeds $257$ million cubes, and the full $p=2$ sweep runs to billions. Unlike the odd-prime cases, the fixed-block dedup gives no reduction here (every orbit has a distinct fix-adjacency pattern), so all cubes must be processed. This is a distributed-compute job, not a desktop one — the subject of Chapter 5.

What "$p=2$ done" would buy

Finish the involution sweep with zero matrices and the theorem upgrades to its strongest form: no $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ has any nontrivial automorphism. A graph, if it exists at all, would then have to be completely rigid — which is exactly the case Chapter 4 confronts. Find a matrix instead, lift it, and the existence question is settled the other way.
