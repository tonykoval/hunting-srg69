A.2   Glossary

Strongly regular graph (SRG). A $k$-regular graph on $n$ vertices in which adjacent vertices share $\lambda$ and non-adjacent vertices share $\mu$ common neighbours.

Open parameter set. One that passes every known non-existence test but has no known construction. $(69,20,7,5)$ was the smallest open SRG existence question in Brouwer's table until the nonexistence proof of Chapter 7 (2026-09-26; write-up under review).

Prescribed automorphism. An assumed symmetry $\sigma$ of prime order $p$; the search is restricted to graphs fixed by $\sigma$, which collapses to a small orbit matrix.

Orbit matrix. The quotient of the adjacency matrix by a prescribed automorphism — a $b\times b$ integer matrix ($b=f+o$) satisfying the Behbahani–Lam column-orbit equations (Ch 2). Zero orbit matrices ⇒ no graph with that symmetry.

Lift. Reconstructing a full $69\times69$ adjacency matrix from an orbit matrix by filling in consistent within-orbit circulant blocks. A successful lift is a genuine SRG.

Cube. A fixed partial assignment splitting the orbit-matrix / SAT search; the unit of distributed work (CUBE_RANGE).

Cayley graph / partial difference set (PDS). $\mathrm{Cay}(G,S)$ is an SRG iff $S\subseteq G$ is a PDS. For order $69$ the only group is $\mathbb{Z}_{69}$; no PDS exists.

Euclidean representation / Gram filter. Vertices map to the $5$-eigenspace ($\dim 23$) as unit vectors; the Gram matrix is PSD of rank $23$ with inner products fixed by adjacency. A partial graph whose forced Gram matrix is not PSD of rank $\le 23$ cannot extend — a sound pruning oracle for the rigid case.

Brouwer's table. A. E. Brouwer's online parameter table of strongly regular graphs; "$?$" marks unknown existence.

The iron law (of this series). A strongly regular graph that is both large and nearly symmetry-free forces full enumeration cost: symmetry-based methods reach only symmetric graphs, and raw search drowns at low symmetry. It is the common reason all three Hunting SRG targets resist every general method. For $(69,20,7,5)$ the proof of Chapter 7 went around it by listing lattices instead of searching graphs.

Configuration / host (Ch 7). A configuration is a family of $69$ vectors with Gram matrix $3I+A+J$ for an $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$. A host is a lattice containing one. The nonexistence theorem says no configuration exists.

Niemeier lattice. An even unimodular lattice of rank $24$. There are $24$: the Leech lattice, which has no roots, and $23$ determined by their root systems.

Discriminant form. For an even lattice $N$, the quadratic form $q_N(x)=|x|^2 \bmod 2$ on $D(N)=N^{\#}/N$. Positive definite even lattices with the same rank and discriminant form lie in one genus. A maximal even lattice has an anisotropic discriminant form.

Pool (Ch 7). The finite set of lattice vectors that could be vertices once a host and $u$ are fixed. The final search picks $69$ of them.

Must-find control. A run of a search tool on a known object of the same shape, which the tool must recover before its "nothing found" on the real target is believed.

DRAT proof. A clausal certificate emitted by a SAT solver alongside an UNSAT verdict, checkable by an independent program (drat-trim).
