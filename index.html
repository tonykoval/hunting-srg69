An Interactive Hunt Log Hunting SRG(69)

Does a strongly regular graph with parameters $(69, 20, 7, 5)$ exist? Nobody knows. It is the smallest set of parameters in the strongly-regular-graph tables for which the question of existence — not count — is still open. This book is the record of the hunt to settle it.

The one-sentence problem

A strongly regular graph $\mathrm{SRG}(n,k,\lambda,\mu)$ is a $k$-regular graph on $n$ vertices in which adjacent vertices share exactly $\lambda$ common neighbours and non-adjacent vertices share exactly $\mu$. The tuple $(69,20,7,5)$ passes every feasibility test ever devised, yet no one has built such a graph or proved one cannot exist.

The motivating question

Brouwer's table lists $(69,20,7,5)$ with a bare "?". Two neighbours were recently settled — $\mathrm{SRG}(65,32,15,16)$ constructed by Gritsenko (2021), $\mathrm{SRG}(85,14,3,2)$ refuted by Shpectorov–Zhao (2025) — which makes $(69,20,7,5)$ the smallest open existence question left in the table. Find one graph and it exists; rule out every possibility and it does not. Either ending is a result.

What this book is

An honest, reproducible hunt log. Unlike a brute attack on $2^{2346}$ graphs, the strategy exploits a key asymmetry of an existence question: we may assume a symmetry, search for a graph that has it, and rule it out if none appears. That engine — the orbit-matrix method — has already closed most of the symmetric search space. What it has proven is as much the content of this book as what it has left open.

Already nailed shut

No $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ has an automorphism of order $3$, $5$, $23$, or any odd prime, and none is a Cayley graph — each a verifiable, exhaustive result (Appendix B). What remains is two precisely-bounded, cluster-scale regions: involution-symmetric graphs and fully rigid ones.

One question, three shapes

This is one of three linked hunt logs. The same object — a strongly regular graph — poses three genuinely different questions, and the same wall stops every general method on each: a target that is large and nearly symmetry-free forces full enumeration cost. That common lesson, met from three angles, is the spine of the series.

The Hunting SRG series
  • SRG(37,18,8,9) — completeness: is the catalog of $6802$ conference graphs final, or is there a $6803$rd?
  • SRG(69,20,7,5) — existence: the smallest parameters with no known graph and no proof.  (you are here)
  • SRG(99,14,1,2) — existence, famous: Conway's $\$1000$ graph.
  • Hunting the Unfinished — archaeology: finishing the "?" rows a 2011 thesis left behind.

Map of the book

Part I — The Target. What $(69,20,7,5)$ is, its spectrum $\{20,5^{23},(-3)^{45}\}$, why it survives every feasibility test (Ch 1), and the orbit-matrix reduction that makes the symmetric search finite (Ch 2).

Part II — The Hunt. The automorphism sweep — every odd prime ruled out, the order-$23$ theorem, the open $p=2$ frontier (Ch 3) — and the Cayley closure plus the rigid Gram-PSD wall (Ch 4).

Part III — Findings & Frontier. What is proven, the two remaining regions, the decision procedure, and the distributed plan that would settle it either way (Ch 5); and the exact commands that reproduce every closure and drive the open regions (Ch 6).
