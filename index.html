An Interactive Hunt Log Hunting SRG(69)

Status (2026-09-26): the hunt is over — no such graph exists

Theorem. There is no strongly regular graph with parameters $(69,20,7,5)$. Consequently there is no quasi-symmetric $2$-$(46,16,8)$ design with block intersection numbers $4$ and $6$.

The proof is computer-assisted and assumes no symmetry. It embeds the graph in a lattice, lists every lattice that could hold it, and closes the last cases with two independent solver engines, one of them producing DRAT-checked UNSAT proofs. It was banked in the project after two independent audits and a review of the write-up. The write-up (docs/paper/srg69.tex in the orbit-gen repository) is being prepared for review by a mathematician. It has not been published or peer-reviewed. Read the outline in Chapter 7 — The End of the Hunt.

Does a strongly regular graph with parameters $(69, 20, 7, 5)$ exist? When this book began, nobody knew. It was the smallest set of parameters in the strongly-regular-graph tables for which the question of existence — not count — was still open. This book is the record of the hunt to settle it, and of how it ended: no such graph exists.

The one-sentence problem

A strongly regular graph $\mathrm{SRG}(n,k,\lambda,\mu)$ is a $k$-regular graph on $n$ vertices in which adjacent vertices share exactly $\lambda$ common neighbours and non-adjacent vertices share exactly $\mu$. The tuple $(69,20,7,5)$ passes every standard feasibility test, yet for years no one had built such a graph or proved that none can exist. Chapter 7 gives the proof that none exists.

The motivating question (as it stood when the hunt began)

Brouwer's table listed $(69,20,7,5)$ with a bare "?". Two neighbours were recently settled — $\mathrm{SRG}(65,32,15,16)$ constructed by Gritsenko (2021), $\mathrm{SRG}(85,14,3,2)$ refuted by Shpectorov–Zhao (2025) — which made $(69,20,7,5)$ the smallest open existence question left in the table. Find one graph and it exists; rule out every possibility and it does not. Either ending is a result. The hunt ended the second way.

What this book is

An honest, reproducible hunt log. Unlike a brute attack on $2^{2346}$ graphs, the strategy exploits a key asymmetry of an existence question: we may assume a symmetry, search for a graph that has it, and rule it out if none appears. That engine — the orbit-matrix method — closed much of the symmetric search space. Chapters 1–6 record what it proved and where it stalled. The ending came from a different direction: a lattice argument that needs no symmetry at all (Chapter 7).

Before the ending (historical)

While the question was open, the hunt excluded symmetric cases one by one. It found no automorphism of order $5$, $23$ or any prime $\ge 7$ (largely re-verifying Behbahani–Lam), and no Cayley graph. The order-$3$ case was downgraded in Chapter 3 to a re-derivation in progress. Two cluster-scale regions were left: involution-symmetric graphs and fully rigid ones. None of this is used in the final proof, and all of it is now a consequence of the nonexistence theorem.

One question, three shapes

This is one of three linked hunt logs. The same object — a strongly regular graph — poses three genuinely different questions, and the same wall stops every general method on each: a target that is large and nearly symmetry-free forces full enumeration cost. That common lesson, met from three angles, is the spine of the series. For $(69,20,7,5)$ the wall was never broken. The proof went around it by counting lattices instead of graphs.

The Hunting SRG series
  • SRG(37,18,8,9) — completeness: is the catalog of $6802$ conference graphs final, or is there a $6803$rd?
  • SRG(69,20,7,5) — existence: once the smallest open case; settled 2026-09-26, no such graph exists (computer-assisted proof, write-up under review).  (you are here)
  • SRG(99,14,1,2) — existence, famous: Conway's $\$1000$ graph.
  • Hunting the Unfinished — archaeology: finishing the "?" rows a 2011 thesis left behind.

Map of the book

Part I — The Target. What $(69,20,7,5)$ is, its spectrum $\{20,5^{23},(-3)^{45}\}$, why it survives every feasibility test (Ch 1), and the orbit-matrix reduction that makes the symmetric search finite (Ch 2).

Part II — The Hunt. The automorphism sweep — every odd prime ruled out, the order-$23$ theorem, the open $p=2$ frontier (Ch 3) — and the Cayley closure plus the rigid Gram-PSD wall (Ch 4).

Part III — Findings & Frontier. What was proven, the two regions left open, the decision procedure, and the distributed plan that would have settled it either way (Ch 5); the exact commands that reproduce every closure and drive the open regions (Ch 6); and the ending: the lattice proof that no $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ exists, what is proved by hand, what is computed, and how it was checked (Ch 7).

Chapters 1–6 were written while the question was open and are kept as the record of the hunt, each with a note pointing to Chapter 7.
