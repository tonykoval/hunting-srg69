Chapter 1 · Part I The Target

Historical: superseded by the nonexistence theorem, Ch. 7

This chapter was written while the existence of $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ was open, and is kept as the record of the hunt. On 2026-09-26 the question was settled: no such graph exists (Chapter 7; computer-assisted proof, write-up under review). The parameters, spectrum and feasibility tests below remain correct. What changed is the "still open" of §1.3.

$\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ is the smallest set of strongly-regular-graph parameters for which nobody knows whether even a single graph exists. Not "how many" — whether any. This book is the record of the hunt to settle it, in either direction.

1.1   The parameters

A graph is $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ if it has $69$ vertices, is $20$-regular, every two adjacent vertices share exactly $\lambda=7$ common neighbours, and every two non-adjacent vertices share exactly $\mu=5$. That is the whole specification. The arithmetic that any SRG must satisfy, $(n-k-1)\mu = k(k-\lambda-1)$, checks out: $\;(69-20-1)\cdot 5 = 48\cdot 5 = 240 = 20\cdot 12 = k(k-\lambda-1)$. So the parameters are feasible at the counting level. They are also feasible at every deeper level we know how to test — which is exactly why the question is hard.

1.2   The spectrum

The non-principal eigenvalues solve $x^2 - (\lambda-\mu)x - (k-\mu) = x^2 - 2x - 15 = 0$, giving $r=5$ and $s=-3$. Their multiplicities follow from $1+f+g=69$ and $20 + 5f - 3g = \mathrm{tr}(A) = 0$:

Spectrum of any SRG(69,20,7,5)

$\mathrm{spec}(A) = \{\,20^{1},\ \ 5^{23},\ \ (-3)^{45}\,\}.$

Both multiplicities are integers ($f=23$, $g=45$) — the integrality condition, the first hurdle most parameter sets fail. This one clears it, and the eigenvalues are integers, so $(69,20,7,5)$ is not even a conference graph; it is an ordinary integral SRG with no built-in symmetry to exploit.

1.3   Why it is still open

A parameter set is "open" when it survives every non-existence test yet no construction is known. $(69,20,7,5)$ survives them all:

The question, precisely

Brouwer's table of strongly regular graphs lists $(69,20,7,5)$ with a bare "?". Two graphs of nearby open parameters were recently settled — $\mathrm{SRG}(65,32,15,16)$ constructed by Gritsenko (2021), $\mathrm{SRG}(85,14,3,2)$ refuted by Shpectorov–Zhao (2025) — leaving $(69,20,7,5)$ as the smallest open existence question in the table. Does one exist? Nobody knows. This is the genuine article: an unsolved problem, not a bookkeeping gap.

1.4   How this differs from a counting hunt

This book has a sister, Hunting SRG(37), which asks whether a known catalog of $6802$ graphs is complete. That is a counting question, and its target is provably rigid, so symmetry-based methods are useless there. Here the situation is opposite and, in one respect, friendlier: because we are asking does any exist, we may freely assume a symmetry, search for a graph that has it, and — if we find none — rule that symmetry out. Chapter 2 turns this into the engine that has already closed most of the symmetric search space.

The two outcomes of the hunt are equally valuable:

The two endings

Existence. One orbit matrix that lifts, or one valid adjacency matrix, and $\mathrm{SRG}(69,20,7,5)$ exists — the first graph ever on these parameters, resolving the smallest open case.
Non-existence. Every automorphism order ruled out and the fully-asymmetric space exhausted, and the parameters are refuted — a result in the line of Shpectorov–Zhao.
